---
title: "Mesh"
description: "Join nodes in different clouds, behind NAT or at home into one private WireGuard network, with no inbound ports."
---

If all your nodes sit on one private network, you do not need a mesh. The
default is `none`, and swarmy sets up no network.

Once nodes live in different clouds, behind NAT, or at home, turn on the
**mesh**. It joins them into one encrypted WireGuard network. The Docker Swarm
overlay then runs across it, so services in one place can reach services in
another.

## Drivers

Pick a driver under **Networking**:

| Driver | What it is |
|---|---|
| `none` | Default. swarmy provisions no mesh. |
| NetBird | Zero-trust WireGuard mesh with NAT traversal. Use NetBird's cloud or your own NetBird server. |
| Headscale (advanced) | Your own Headscale server (the open Tailscale control plane). Nodes run the official Tailscale client. |

The control-plane token you paste is stored encrypted and used only on the
server. You do not see it again.

## Enrolling a node

Pick a node and click **Enroll**. swarmy mints a single-use, short-lived setup key
and sends it to the agent over its existing connection. The agent starts the
WireGuard client, and the node joins with no inbound ports and no firewall
changes. The key is never written to disk.

The node then appears in the peer list with its mesh IP, last handshake and
status.

## Direct connect

Need to reach a database from your laptop? On the service, click **Direct
connect** (available on drivers with an access-control API, such as NetBird). swarmy gives you a command that joins your laptop to the mesh as a
temporary peer that can reach only that one service, for a number of minutes you
choose. You connect to the service on its real port with no public exposure.
Every direct connection is audited.

## Add a destination

Open **Backup destinations** (in the sidebar under Platform) and click **Add
destination**. Choose one:

- **S3**: any S3-compatible bucket, such as AWS, Backblaze B2, Cloudflare R2 or
  MinIO. Enter the endpoint, bucket and keys.
- **Node path**: a directory on one of your nodes, such as `/srv/backups`.
- **swarmy object storage**: one click creates a bucket on the in-swarm Garage
  store. Turn on the replicated store on the same page first.

Destinations are shared by every stack in the organisation. Credentials are
encrypted and only decrypted in memory when a backup runs.

## What gets backed up

Once a destination exists, databases are backed up **nightly with no further
clicks**:

- **Managed Postgres** gets a nightly `pg_dump`. You can also turn on WAL
  archiving for point-in-time recovery.
- **Databases in your own compose or templates**, such as MariaDB, MySQL,
  Postgres, MongoDB and Redis/Valkey, get a nightly volume backup, as long as
  they sit on a named volume.

Each keeps 7 backups by default. Runs are spread between 02:00 and 05:00 UTC. A
schedule you set yourself is never overridden.

You can also schedule backups for any other named volume.

Every backup is an encrypted, deduplicated **restic** repository. A bucket full of
restic snapshots can be restored by hand without swarmy.

## Restore

Open the stack's **Backups** tab, pick a snapshot and click **Restore**.

- Keep the volume name to restore **in place**. This overwrites that volume.
- Give a new volume name to restore **next to** the original.

Managed Postgres can also be restored to a new cluster, as a single database, or
to a point in time.

A failed backup shows its reason on the snapshot row.

## Drills

A backup you have never restored is untested. Drills rehearse a restore safely: on
a throwaway cluster, or as a verify of a backup's integrity. Every drill is
recorded, and the resilience score shows when each backup was last tested.

## Back up the controller

The controller can back itself up into one encrypted bundle: its database, its
secrets and its config. The bundle is sealed with a **restore passphrase** that
only you hold. It is shown once, so write it down.

A controller restored from this bundle on a fresh server takes over the existing
swarm. The agents reconnect by themselves, and no node has to be enrolled again.

## When the controller is down

`swarmy-agent backup export` copies a stack's volumes off a node with no
controller at all. See [Node recovery](/docs/guides/node-recovery#rescue-backups-controller-down-safe).
